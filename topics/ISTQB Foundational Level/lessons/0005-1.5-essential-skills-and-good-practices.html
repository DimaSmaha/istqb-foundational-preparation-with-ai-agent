1.5 Essential Skills and Good Practices in Testing

Closes out Chapter 1: what makes a good tester, and a tension the exam likes to probe — whole-team collaboration vs. independence.

FL-1.5.1 (K2) Give examples of the generic skills required for testing FL-1.5.2 (K1) Recall the advantages of the whole team approach FL-1.5.3 (K2) Distinguish the benefits and drawbacks of independence of testing

Generic skills required for testing (FL-1.5.1)

Skill = the ability to do something well, from knowledge, practice, and aptitude. Relevant tester skills:

  • Testing knowledge — using test techniques to increase effectiveness
  • Thoroughness, carefulness, curiosity, attention to detail, being methodical — for finding hard-to-find defects
  • Good communication, active listening, being a team player — testers are often "bearers of bad news"; confirmation bias can make people resist hearing it, so constructive communication matters
  • Analytical thinking, critical thinking, creativity
  • Technical knowledge — for using tools effectively
  • Domain knowledge — to understand and communicate with business/end users

Whole team approach (FL-1.5.2)

Coming from Extreme Programming, in the whole team approach any team member with the right knowledge and skills can perform any task, and everyone is responsible for quality. Teams share a workspace (physical or virtual) to facilitate communication.

Advantages: improved team dynamics, better communication and collaboration, synergy from leveraging varied skill sets. Testers still bring value here — they transfer testing knowledge to the team, help business representatives write acceptance tests, and work with developers on test strategy and automation approach.

It's not always appropriate — safety-critical contexts may need a high level of test independence instead.

Independence of testing (FL-1.5.3)

A certain degree of independence makes testers more effective at finding defects, because the author's and the tester's cognitive biases differ. It is not a replacement for familiarity — developers can efficiently find many defects in their own code.

Most projects use multiple levels at once — e.g. developers doing component/component integration testing, an independent test team doing system/system integration testing, business representatives doing acceptance testing.

Benefits

  • Recognizes different kinds of failures/defects than developers, due to differing backgrounds and biases
  • Can verify, challenge, or disprove assumptions made during specification and implementation

Drawbacks

  • Isolation from the dev team → communication problems, adversarial relationships
  • Developers may feel less responsible for quality
  • Independent testers can be blamed as a bottleneck for release delays
