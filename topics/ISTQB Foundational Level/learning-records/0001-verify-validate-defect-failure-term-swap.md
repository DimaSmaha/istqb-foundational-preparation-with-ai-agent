# Verify/Validate and Defect/Failure term-swap is the learner's recurring error mode

The learner has correct *conceptual* understanding of verification vs. validation and the error→defect→failure chain — both are stated accurately in their own §1.1 notes — but missed two Chapter 1 quiz questions (lesson 0001: Exam C·Q1 and Exam C·Q2) specifically because the wrong option swapped in the *adjacent* near-synonym term rather than testing a different concept: "validating that documented requirements are met" (validate ↔ verify swap) and "debugging fixes failures" (failure ↔ defect swap). This means the learner's failure mode isn't missing knowledge, it's not yet reflexively checking which precise term an option uses against which precise term the syllabus objective/definition actually uses.

A third occurrence confirms the pattern extends beyond term pairs to *shared keywords*: on the §1.3 "absence-of-defects fallacy" question, both wrong picks described principle #1 ("testing shows the presence, not the absence, of defects") instead of principle #7, which is *named* the absence-of-defects fallacy. Two different principles sharing the phrase "absence of defects" made it easy to match on the words rather than the concept — the same lexical-matching reflex as the verify/validate and defect/failure swaps.

**Implications:** future review of any chapter with paired near-synonym ISTQB terms (verify/validate, defect/failure, error/mistake, and later risk-based/requirement-based, etc.) or with a keyword shared across two distinct concepts should include "spot the swapped term" style drills, not just concept-recall questions — this is the learner's specific zone of proximal development gap, not a general weak spot in Chapter 1.
